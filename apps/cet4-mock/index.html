Sample: University gives students far more freedom than high school, and how we use that freedom depends largely on our study habits. First, a fixed daily routine helps us manage time well. When we study at the same hours every day, focusing becomes easier and deadlines feel less stressful. Second, reviewing lecture notes on the same day turns short-term memory into lasting knowledge, which saves a great deal of time before exams. Third, good habits include knowing when to put the phone away. Constant notifications break our attention and make an hour of study worth only twenty minutes. In short, good study habits are not about working harder but about working smarter. If we build them early, they will benefit us long after we graduate.

Section C · 仔细阅读（Questions 19–23）

In recent years, second-hand shopping has moved from the edges of the market to its center. Apps that let people resell clothes, books and electronics now attract millions of users, and young buyers are leading the trend. Some economists have read this as a sign of financial pressure on the younger generation. Surveys, however, tell a more complicated story. Many buyers say they choose used goods because they want to reduce waste, or because they enjoy finding items that no one else has.

Retailers have noticed. Several clothing brands now buy back their own products from customers, clean them, and sell them again in special sections of their stores. For these companies, resale is a way to keep customers loyal and to present a greener image.

Critics warn that the boom may not be as green as it looks. Cheap delivery encourages people to buy more often, and some resold items travel thousands of kilometers before reaching a new owner. The real environmental benefit, they argue, depends on one simple question: does a second-hand purchase replace a new one, or does it simply add to what people consume?

参考译文

In recent years, more and more young people in China have chosen to go on a city walk at weekends. Instead of chasing popular tourist attractions, they slow down and wander through old streets and alleys to experience local history and daily life. This way of relaxing costs little and is easy to start. It not only helps people keep fit but also relieves stress. Many cities have taken this opportunity to launch walking routes, which has boosted small shops and cultural venues along the way. Experts believe that city walks reflect young people's new understanding of the quality of life.
