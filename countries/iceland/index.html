Study in Iceland: Complete Guide

Check language, programme, budget and residence rules before deciding

Iceland's broader English-taught provision is concentrated at master's and doctoral level; English-taught bachelor's choices are comparatively limited. OTC screens the named programme rather than the destination image, checking teaching language, academic fit, costs, residence and graduate outcomes.

Four facts to know first

Iceland has no central university application system. Each institution sets its own programmes, deadlines, language rules and fees.

01Seven universities

Iceland has seven universities across comprehensive research, technology, business, arts, agriculture, marine and regional studies.

02Fewer English bachelor's options

Most undergraduate degrees are taught in Icelandic. English provision is broader at graduate level and must be checked for the relevant academic year.

03Three- to four-year bachelor's degrees

Bachelor's degrees are generally 180–240 ECTS over three to four years. Full-time student residence normally means 30 ECTS per semester.

04Apply to each institution

There is no central system. International deadlines often fall in spring, but exact dates, evidence and application fees are programme-specific.

Workflow

Five checks from idea to enrolment

STEP 01Profile review

Organise qualifications, full grades, subject prerequisites, employment, English or Icelandic, budget and intended intake.

STEP 02Programme and language

Shortlist only programmes open in the relevant year and mark prerequisites, portfolios, research proposals or interview requirements.

STEP 03Application evidence

Prepare passport, transcripts, completion evidence, language results, CV, motivation, references and programme-specific materials.

STEP 04Apply directly

There is no central admissions system. Submit to each institution, pay applicable fees, respond to evidence requests and check whether an offer is unconditional.

STEP 05Residence and arrival

After admission, non-EEA/EFTA students prepare funds, insurance, criminal-record evidence and student residence; start housing searches in parallel.

DOCUMENTS

Documents for screening and formal application

01Academic evidence

Full transcript, enrolment or completion evidence, degree certificate, grading scale and any required official translation.

02Language evidence

IELTS, TOEFL or an accepted alternative; Icelandic-taught bachelor's degrees require the programme's Icelandic evidence.

03Application narrative

CV, motivation and referee details; research programmes may need a proposal, while arts programmes may require a portfolio or audition.

04Residence evidence

Unconditional full-time admission, valid passport, photograph, criminal record, health insurance and verifiable proof of maintenance funds.

COSTS · 2026/27

Costs and budget: look beyond 'public tuition-free'

01Public and private differ

Study in Iceland states that public universities generally charge registration or administration fees, while private institutions may charge tuition. Status and programme matter.

02University of Iceland 2026/27

The annual registration fee is ISK 100,000 and new non-EEA/EFTA applicants pay an ISK 20,000 processing fee. Autumn 2026 starters are not charged tuition.

03Change may begin in 2027

The government has authorised public universities to introduce tuition for non-EEA/EFTA and Swiss students from autumn 2027. University of Iceland rates remain to be determined.

04Residence funds are not the full budget

The 2026 student-residence minimum is ISK 259,951 per month. It is an official threshold, not an all-in quote for rent, deposit, transport, insurance and personal spending.

05Check scholarships institution by institution

Awards are mainly offered by individual universities. A prominent Icelandic government scheme for foreign students focuses on Icelandic language, literature and history, so general full-degree funding should not be assumed.

RESIDENCE · WORK

Student residence, work and post-study status are separate

01Student residence

Non-EEA/EFTA applicants normally need unconditional full-time admission at 30 ECTS per semester, plus passport, criminal-record, insurance and financial evidence.

02Work while studying

Under rules announced in June 2026, valid student-residence holders may work up to 60% of full-time during study and full-time in scheduled breaks, without a separate work permit. Self-employment remains prohibited.

03When work can start

A first-time applicant cannot begin employment until the student residence permit has been issued. Employment is reported at renewal and excess hours can affect work rights.

04After graduation

Graduates of an Icelandic bachelor's, master's or doctorate may qualify for a job-seeking residence route as a bridge to specialist employment. It does not guarantee a job or permanent residence.

ARRIVAL

Housing, healthcare, language and arrival

01Housing

Student housing exists near many universities but is not guaranteed. Contact the institution and student housing as soon as admitted, with a private-rental fallback.

02Healthcare

EU/EEA students normally bring an EHIC. Non-EU students need compliant insurance before arrival; national coverage begins automatically after three months of registered legal domicile.

03Icelandic

English is widely used, but Icelandic is the official language. Local services, education, healthcare and longer-term careers can depend materially on Icelandic ability.

04Climate and location

Reykjavík concentrates services and opportunities; Akureyri, Bifröst and Hólar offer different scales. Winter daylight, weather, transport and placement locations belong in the decision.
