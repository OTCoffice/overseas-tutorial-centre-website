Czech Nursing Bachelor Guide 2026:
English-Taught Reality, Costs, and a Czech–Hungary–Poland Shortlist

Czechia can look appealing — but Nursing has a language reality

Czechia often looks attractive to nursing applicants because the overall cost can be lower than Western Europe and the living environment is usually strong. The practical question, however, is whether the route is actually delivered in English from classroom to placement.

For families, the useful check is simple: confirm teaching language, assessment language and clinical placement language separately, then get the admissions office to confirm the position for your intake in writing.

Which Czech universities are worth checking first

University of South Bohemia is one of the clearest Czech references because its Institute of Nursing, Midwifery and Emergency Care explicitly states that the bachelor’s Nurse route is accredited in an English version. That gives families a real English-track lead rather than a vague departmental signal.

University of Ostrava remains worth checking because the nursing department is practice-oriented and established, but families should still verify exactly what is offered in English for the intended intake. High-prestige names such as Charles University and Masaryk University should also be treated as routes to verify carefully rather than assumed English options.

IELTS 6.0 can open conversations, but not every door equally

IELTS 6.0 is often enough to begin serious admissions conversations in Central Europe, especially where schools work around a B2 expectation. But Nursing is communication-heavy, so applicants should still expect closer checking of science background, motivation, interview performance and placement readiness.

If a school mentions a possible waiver based on prior English-medium study or internal testing, get the exact rule in writing for that intake year before relying on it.

Czechia, Hungary and Poland should be compared on one matrix

Hungary is easier to defend when a family wants a mature English nursing route, because Semmelweis publicly lists an English Nursing BSc with a clear duration and fee structure. Poland also tends to offer more visible English nursing options than Czechia, which is why many budget-conscious families find it easier to build a shortlist there.

The useful matrix is not built on reputation alone. It should compare language of instruction, placement language, tuition, city cost, science-entry fit and the recognition path the family expects after graduation.

OTC’s practical reading of the three-country choice

If budget and ease of shortlisting come first, Poland is often easier to start with. If families want the strongest single brand for an English-language nursing route, Hungary is easier to explain. If the family prefers Czechia for quality of life or a calmer city rhythm, then University of South Bohemia is the first place to inspect carefully.

The deciding factors stay the same in all three countries: teaching language, placement language, entry requirements and the real document list. Those four checks matter more than hearsay.
